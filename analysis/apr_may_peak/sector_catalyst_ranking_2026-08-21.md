# Apr–May Peak Cohort: Sector/Catalyst Ranking — 2026-08-21

This note extends `analysis/apr_may_peak/` from waveform classification into fundamental/sector attribution. It is a research ranking, not a guaranteed-return model.

## Decision rule

Primary horizon: 3 months; secondary horizon: 6 months.

A heavy-allocation candidate must pass all four tests:

1. Apr–May rally was supported by measurable revenue/earnings/order growth, not only multiple expansion.
2. Current pullback is substantially sector/macro/financing related; the core demand thesis remains intact.
3. There is a visible 1–3 month catalyst or a near-term earnings path that can force estimate revisions.
4. Current price/location leaves enough upside relative to the risk of another sector de-rating.

## Sector map

### AI infrastructure manufacturing / networking — highest priority

Representative cohort: CLS, FN, CIEN, AAOI.

Apr–May rally driver: hyperscaler AI capex, 800G/1.6T networking, accelerated data-center compute deployment, component/capacity constraints.

Pullback driver: July/August semiconductor and AI-infrastructure de-rating, profit taking, scrutiny of hyperscaler capex sustainability, higher long-term bond yields. Company-specific dilution or margin issues then amplify individual names.

Conclusion: distinguish firms whose forward demand/earnings estimates are still rising from firms with real margin/cash-flow deterioration.

### Semiconductors / memory interface — high priority

Representative cohort: NXPI, RMBS, QCOM, CRUS.

Apr–May rally driver: AI infrastructure + physical AI/automotive recovery + memory bandwidth requirements.

Pullback driver: broad semiconductor multiple compression and AI-capex concerns. QCOM also has a real Apple/handset transition; therefore its drawdown is not purely sector-driven.

Conclusion: NXPI and RMBS have cleaner fundamental setups than QCOM for a 3-month recovery thesis.

### Power / solar / storage — selective high priority

Representative cohort: NXT, FSLR, ENPH, SEDG, SHLS.

Apr–May rally driver: U.S. power scarcity, data-center load growth, tax-credit deadline rush, solar-plus-storage deployment and 'time-to-power' economics.

Pullback driver: accelerated expiry of U.S. renewable credits, tariff/policy risk and broad risk-off conditions. The demand backdrop remains strong, but policy sensitivity differs sharply by company.

Conclusion: NXT is preferred because backlog is large and the platform is broadening into storage and power conversion; pure solar exposure carries more policy risk.

### Commercial space — lower confidence for heavy allocation

Representative cohort: RKLB, LUNR, PL, RDW, BKSY.

Apr–May rally driver included a major sector valuation re-rating around the SpaceX IPO narrative in addition to company fundamentals.

Pullback driver included post-IPO profit taking / narrative unwind. The large drawdowns therefore do not automatically represent fundamental undervaluation.

Conclusion: suitable for event-driven positions, but lower-confidence as a heavy core position.

### Materials / cyclicals — lower priority

Representative cohort: ALB, AA, CENX, WLK, DOW.

Returns depend heavily on commodity cycles, trade policy and industrial demand. Their Apr–May peaks and later pullbacks are less directly linked to a single durable AI infrastructure earnings engine.

## Heavy-allocation research ranking

### 1. CLS — highest conviction

Wave position: G2, 27.9% below Apr–May peak and approximately flat vs January on the 2026-08-20 archive.

Fundamentals: Q2 revenue $4.70B, +62% YoY; adjusted EPS $2.54 vs $1.39. 2026 revenue outlook raised to $20.5B (+65%) and adjusted EPS to $11.30 (+87%). Management expects 2027 revenue growth to accelerate beyond 2026 and adjusted EPS to grow faster than revenue.

Capacity signal: Celestica raised approximately $3.45B at $310/share in August specifically to fund working capital/capex supporting 'unprecedented multi-year demand'. Fort Worth expansion exceeds one million square feet and targets about 1,700 jobs.

Interpretation of pullback: broad AI-infrastructure de-rating plus equity dilution. This is materially different from a demand collapse: estimates were raised and capital is being raised to add capacity.

Latest verified 2026-08-21 price used for current context: about $298.59, below the recent $310 public offering price. The offering price is not a price floor.

3-month scenario: +20% to +35% if Q3 tracks guidance and the market accepts the capacity raise as growth-funded rather than defensive. Confidence: high.

Main invalidation: customer demand/forecast cuts, failure to convert capex into 2027 program ramps, or margin reversal.

### 2. NXPI — highest-quality recovery

Wave position: G2, 30.6% below peak and 4.1% below January median on the 2026-08-20 archive.

Fundamentals: Q2 revenue $3.50B, +19% YoY and +10% sequential, with growth across all end markets and regions. Management identifies SDV, Physical AI and Data Center as company-specific growth engines.

Interpretation of pullback: shares fell into a broad semiconductor de-rating despite strong operating results. This is a cleaner 'sector compression > thesis damage' case than QCOM.

3-month scenario: +15% to +30% if sector multiples stabilize and Q3 confirms the +18%-ish growth trajectory. Confidence: high.

Main invalidation: automotive/industrial demand rollover or lower data-center contribution than management expects.

### 3. NXT — best risk/reward in power infrastructure

Wave position: G3, 43.8% below peak and 8.8% below January median on the 2026-08-20 archive.

Fundamentals: Q1 FY27 revenue $935M vs $864M YoY; gross margin 35.9%; adjusted EBITDA $233M. Backlog exceeds $5.5B, with Prevalon adding significantly above $300M. FY27 revenue outlook is $4.1–4.4B and adjusted EPS $4.42–4.73.

Platform catalyst: Prevalon energy storage, Apex/Zigor power-conversion assets and international tracker expansion move the company from a tracker-only story toward an integrated clean-power platform.

Sector catalyst: U.S. power demand is setting records and 'time to power' is becoming the limiting factor for data-center development; solar-plus-storage remains among the fastest deployable new supply options.

Interpretation of pullback: policy/tax-credit uncertainty and acquisition/execution discount are real, but current backlog and raised operating guidance argue against a demand-collapse interpretation.

At the 2026-08-20 close of ~$87.86, FY27 adjusted EPS guidance midpoint implies roughly 19x forward adjusted EPS.

3-month scenario: +20% to +40%. Confidence: medium-high.

Main invalidation: post-tax-credit project cancellations, tariff/commodity pressure, or acquisition integration lowering margins/bookings.

### 4. RMBS — higher-upside AI memory bottleneck

Wave position: G3, 41.8% below peak and 14.7% below January median on the 2026-08-20 archive.

Fundamentals: Q2 revenue $207.4M, +20% YoY; record product revenue $99.2M, +22% YoY and +13% sequential. Q3 product revenue guide rises to $110–116M. Cash/securities were $824.9M.

Product catalyst: DDR5-9600 server RDIMM chipset, client memory chipset and PCIe 7 IP directly target memory/interconnect bottlenecks in AI/data-center systems.

Interpretation of pullback: broad semiconductor de-rating dominates the thesis; operating/product momentum remains positive. Valuation sensitivity is higher than NXPI, so confidence is lower despite greater upside elasticity.

3-month scenario: +20% to +40%. Confidence: medium-high.

Main invalidation: memory-interface product ramp misses Q3 guidance or hyperscaler/server demand materially slows.

### 5. FN — strong business, but wait for proof after earnings reset

Wave position: G2, 38.4% below peak and 7.2% below January median on the 2026-08-20 archive; shares fell sharply again after FY26 results.

Fundamentals: Q4 FY26 revenue $1.316B, +45% YoY; FY26 revenue $4.64B, +36%; FY26 adjusted EPS $14.09 vs $10.17. Q1 FY27 revenue guide $1.375–1.425B and adjusted EPS $4.10–4.25.

Why it ranks below the top four: revenue momentum is excellent, but the market is explicitly questioning near-term profitability/cash conversion. The post-earnings selloff therefore contains more company-specific information than CLS/NXPI/RMBS.

Action: watch for stabilization plus margin/free-cash-flow evidence rather than treat the drawdown alone as a buy signal.

## Exclusions / lower priority

- QCOM: price is almost exactly back to January and diversification is progressing, but weak near-term profit guidance and accelerating Apple revenue loss make the drawdown partly company-specific.
- Space cohort: May highs were influenced by a sector-wide SpaceX/IPO valuation rerating; high drawdown is not enough to establish a high-confidence rebound.
- FSLR / ENPH / SEDG: long-run power demand is strong, but policy/tax-credit and contract-specific risks are larger than for NXT.
- Commodity/material names: potential rebound exists but has lower earnings-path visibility for this specific 3-month framework.

## Practical conclusion

If the objective is simultaneous return potential and confidence, the current order is:

**CLS > NXPI > NXT > RMBS > FN**.

For a two-name research focus, use **CLS + NXPI** for maximum confidence. For more upside elasticity, replace NXPI with **NXT**. RMBS is the preferred smaller aggressive AI-semiconductor position.
